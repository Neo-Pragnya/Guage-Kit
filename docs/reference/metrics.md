# Metric reference

> [!NOTE]
> Generated by `scripts/gen_metric_docs.py` from the metric registry and the metric cards. `guage metrics list`
> and `guage metrics show <name>` print the same information for the installed version.

Guage Kit has **90 native metrics** and exposes **77 Ananke Plexus evaluators**, 167 in total. Each family page documents every metric in full: what it measures, how it
is computed (with the formula), its parameters, how to read the number, when to use it, its pitfalls, a worked
example that the test suite runs, and the papers that define it.

↑ means higher is better and ↓ lower is better. *Sample* metrics score each sample and report the mean with a
bootstrap interval; *corpus* metrics are computed over the whole set, and their interval comes from resampling
the samples. Retrieval metrics take a cut-off: `recall@5`, `ndcg@10`.

## Text

| metric | level | needs | in one line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [`bleu`](metrics/text.md#bleu) ↑ | corpus | `output`, `expected` | How many of the output's word sequences (1 to 4 words long) also appear in the reference, pooled over the whole dataset, with a penalty for outputs that are too short. |
| [`chrf`](metrics/text.md#chrf) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | How many of the output's character sequences (1 to 6 characters long) also appear in the reference, combined into a recall-weighted F-score. |
| [`chrf_pp`](metrics/text.md#chrf_pp) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | chrF with word unigrams and bigrams added to the character n-grams, so word order and whole-word matches count as well. |
| [`contains`](metrics/text.md#contains) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | 1 if any reference text appears anywhere inside the output (case-insensitive substring), otherwise 0. |
| [`corpus_chrf`](metrics/text.md#corpus_chrf) ↑ | corpus | `output`, `expected` | chrF computed once over the whole dataset, by pooling character n-gram matches from every sample before computing the F-score. |
| [`distinct_n`](metrics/text.md#distinct_n) ↑ | corpus | `output` | The share of word n-grams (bigrams by default) across all outputs that are unique; higher means more varied, less repetitive outputs. |
| [`exact_match`](metrics/text.md#exact_match) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | 1 if the output is exactly the same string as a reference (ignoring surrounding whitespace), otherwise 0. |
| [`flesch_reading_ease`](metrics/text.md#flesch_reading_ease) ↑ | sample | `output` | A readability score from average sentence length and average syllables per word; higher means easier to read. |
| [`jaccard`](metrics/text.md#jaccard) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | The share of distinct words used by either text that are used by both (intersection over union of word sets). |
| [`keyword_coverage`](metrics/text.md#keyword_coverage) ↑ | sample | `output` | The fraction of required keywords that appear somewhere in the output. |
| [`length_ratio`](metrics/text.md#length_ratio) ↓ | sample | `output`, `expected` | The output's word count divided by the reference's word count; 1 means the same length. |
| [`levenshtein_similarity`](metrics/text.md#levenshtein_similarity) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | How few single-character edits are needed to turn the output into the closest reference, as a similarity from 0 to 1. |
| [`normalized_match`](metrics/text.md#normalized_match) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | 1 if the output equals a reference after lower-casing and removing punctuation, articles and extra spaces (SQuAD exact match), otherwise 0. |
| [`numeric_match`](metrics/text.md#numeric_match) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | 1 if the last number in the output equals an expected number within a tolerance, otherwise 0; the standard check for math word problems. |
| [`regex_match`](metrics/text.md#regex_match) ↑ | sample | `output` | 1 if the output matches a regular expression, otherwise 0. |
| [`rouge`](metrics/text.md#rouge) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | How much of the reference's wording the output recovers, as unigram, bigram or longest-common-subsequence overlap; the configurable base of rouge1, rouge2, rougeL and rougeLsum. |
| [`rouge1`](metrics/text.md#rouge1) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | Share of individual words the output and the reference have in common, as an F-score (ROUGE-1). |
| [`rouge2`](metrics/text.md#rouge2) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | Share of adjacent word pairs the output and the reference have in common, as an F-score (ROUGE-2). |
| [`rougeL`](metrics/text.md#rougel) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | Length of the longest sequence of words that appears in the same order in both output and reference, as an F-score (ROUGE-L). |
| [`rougeLsum`](metrics/text.md#rougelsum) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | Sentence-aware ROUGE-L for multi-sentence summaries; each reference sentence is matched against every output sentence, so sentence order does not matter. |
| [`self_bleu`](metrics/text.md#self_bleu) ↓ | corpus | `output` | How similar the outputs are to each other, as the average BLEU of each output against all the others; lower means more diverse. |
| [`sentence_bleu`](metrics/text.md#sentence_bleu) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | BLEU for a single output against its own references, smoothed so that a short or partly matching answer still gets a non-zero score. |
| [`token_f1`](metrics/text.md#token_f1) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | Word-overlap F1 between the output and the best-matching reference, treating each text as a bag of words (SQuAD F1). |
| [`within_length`](metrics/text.md#within_length) ↑ | sample | `output` | 1 if the output's word count is between a minimum and maximum (inclusive), otherwise 0. |
| [`word_count`](metrics/text.md#word_count) ↑ | sample | `output` | The number of words in the output, with an optional check against a minimum and maximum. |

## RAG

| metric | level | needs | in one line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [`answer_correctness`](metrics/rag.md#answer_correctness) ↑ | sample | `output`, `expected` | Claim-level F1 between the answer and the reference answer, i.e. how many of the answer's claims the reference backs and how many of the reference's claims the answer covers. |
| [`answer_relevancy`](metrics/rag.md#answer_relevancy) ↑ | sample | `input`, `output` | How directly the answer addresses the question, from embedding similarity when an embedder is available and from word coverage otherwise. |
| [`citation_accuracy`](metrics/rag.md#citation_accuracy) ↑ | sample | `output`, `contexts` | The share of inline citations `[n]` whose cited context actually supports the sentence that cites it. |
| [`citation_coverage`](metrics/rag.md#citation_coverage) ↑ | sample | `output` | The share of answer sentences that carry at least one inline citation `[n]`. |
| [`context_precision`](metrics/rag.md#context_precision) ↑ | sample | `contexts` | Rank-aware precision of the retrieved chunks, which is high when the relevant chunks come before the irrelevant ones. |
| [`context_recall`](metrics/rag.md#context_recall) ↑ | sample | `contexts`, `expected` | The share of the reference answer's claims that the retrieved contexts contain, i.e. whether retrieval found everything needed to answer. |
| [`context_relevance`](metrics/rag.md#context_relevance) ↑ | sample | `input`, `contexts` | The share of sentences in the retrieved contexts that relate to the question, which measures how much of what was retrieved is noise. |
| [`context_utilization`](metrics/rag.md#context_utilization) ↑ | sample | `output`, `contexts` | The share of retrieved chunks that the answer actually draws on, so low values mean context was retrieved but never used. |
| [`faithfulness`](metrics/rag.md#faithfulness) ↑ | sample | `output`, `contexts` | The share of the answer's claims that the retrieved contexts support, so 1.0 means everything the answer says can be found in what was retrieved. |
| [`hallucination_rate`](metrics/rag.md#hallucination_rate) ↓ | sample | `output`, `contexts` | The share of the answer's claims that nothing in the retrieved contexts supports; exactly 1 − faithfulness, so lower is better. |

## Retrieval

| metric | level | needs | in one line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [`average_precision`](metrics/retrieval.md#average_precision) ↑ | sample | `retrieved_ids`, `relevant_ids` or `relevance` | The average of precision@i over the ranks i that hold a relevant id, divided by all relevant ids; the mean over samples is MAP. |
| [`hit_rate`](metrics/retrieval.md#hit_rate) ↑ | sample | `retrieved_ids`, `relevant_ids` or `relevance` | 1 when at least one relevant id appears in the top k retrieved, else 0; the mean over samples is the share of queries with a hit (success@k). |
| [`mrr`](metrics/retrieval.md#mrr) ↑ | sample | `retrieved_ids`, `relevant_ids` or `relevance` | The reciprocal of the rank of the first relevant id (1 at rank 1, 0.5 at rank 2, 0 if none); the mean over samples is mean reciprocal rank. |
| [`ndcg`](metrics/retrieval.md#ndcg) ↑ | sample | `retrieved_ids`, `relevant_ids` or `relevance` | Normalised discounted cumulative gain at k, which rewards putting the most relevant ids first, on a 0–1 scale where 1 is the ideal ordering. |
| [`precision`](metrics/retrieval.md#precision) ↑ | sample | `retrieved_ids`, `relevant_ids` or `relevance` | The share of the top k retrieved ids that are relevant (precision@k). |
| [`r_precision`](metrics/retrieval.md#r_precision) ↑ | sample | `retrieved_ids`, `relevant_ids` or `relevance` | Precision at rank R, where R is the number of relevant ids for the query, so the cut-off adapts to each query. |
| [`recall`](metrics/retrieval.md#recall) ↑ | sample | `retrieved_ids`, `relevant_ids` or `relevance` | The share of the relevant ids that appear in the top k retrieved (recall@k). |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [`counterfactual_consistency`](metrics/safety.md#counterfactual_consistency) ↑ | corpus | `output` | Whether prompts that differ only in a demographic detail (a name, a pronoun, a nationality) get equivalent answers, as the mean token F1 between their outputs. |
| [`injection_resistance`](metrics/safety.md#injection_resistance) ↑ | sample | `output` | 1 when the model did not obey an injected instruction, checked by the absence of the probe's unique canary token in the output. |
| [`pii_leakage`](metrics/safety.md#pii_leakage) ↓ | sample | `output` | 1 when the output contains personal data (email, phone number, US SSN, valid card number, IPv4 address, IBAN) that was not already in the input. Lower is better. |

| [`refusal_correctness`](metrics/safety.md#refusal_correctness) ↑ | sample | `output` | 1 when the model refused exactly when it should have, per `metadata.should_refuse`; catches both over-refusal and under-refusal. |
| [`secret_leakage`](metrics/safety.md#secret_leakage) ↓ | sample | `output` | 1 when the output contains credential-shaped text such as cloud access key ids, private key blocks, platform tokens or `password = …` assignments. Lower is better. |
| [`system_prompt_leakage`](metrics/safety.md#system_prompt_leakage) ↓ | sample | `output` | The share of the system prompt's 8-word sequences that reappear in the output. Lower is better. |
| [`toxicity`](metrics/safety.md#toxicity) ↓ | sample | `output` | The share of the output's sentences that contain an insult, threat, profanity or hostile phrase from a small built-in word list. Lower is better. |
